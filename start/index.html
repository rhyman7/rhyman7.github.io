Betting basics

The arithmetic behind every price on the site.

American odds
How US books write prices. −110 means risk $110 to win $100. +145 means a $100 bet wins $145.
Implied probability
The chance a price needs to break even. −110 needs 52.4%; +145 needs 40.8%.
Vig
The book's cut. Both sides of a spread at −110 add up to 104.8%, and the extra 4.8% is the vig.
No-vig chance
A book's price with the vig taken out, also called the fair chance. Two sides at −110 are 50% each.
Spread
The points a favorite gives. Packers −3 wins the bet only if Green Bay wins by more than 3.
Total
Both teams' points added together, bet over or under a number.
Moneyline
A bet on who wins the game, with no points involved.
ATS
Against the spread. A team that is 7-4 ATS has covered the spread seven times.
Push
A bet that lands exactly on the number. The stake comes back.
Opening line
The first number a book posts for a game, also called the opener. On the matchup pages, the line from the week's first update.
Closing line
The last line before kickoff. It holds everything the market learned during the week, so it is the best single estimate of the game.
Closing line value (CLV)
How much better your price was than the closing price. Beating the close week after week is the earliest sign a method works; a win-loss record takes seasons to say the same thing. The boards measure it against Pinnacle's close.
Pinnacle
A sportsbook with low vig that takes large bets, so its prices are treated as the sharpest in the market. The boards use it as the reference; it is not a book the bets are placed at.
Expected value (EV)
The average profit on a bet if it could be placed many times. +3% EV means about 30 cents per $10 over the long run.
Unit
One standard bet. On this site a unit is $10.
Kelly
A formula that sizes a stake from the edge and the price. The boards use a quarter of it, and cap any one bet, game and week.
Profit boost
A sportsbook promotion that adds a percentage to the winnings. A 30% boost turns a $9.09 win into $11.82.
Parlay
Several bets joined into one. Every leg has to win.
Teaser
A parlay where each spread or total is moved a set number of points in your favor, for a lower payout.
Kalshi
An exchange where a bet is a contract priced from 1 to 99 cents. The site shows Kalshi prices after its fee.

On the value boards

Words used on the NFL and College Value Boards.

Bet sheet
The list of bets a board flags this week, each with the book, line, price and stake.
Tier
Why a bet was flagged. Each tier has its own record.
Price tier
One book's price is better than Pinnacle's no-vig chance for the same side. It needs no opinion from the model.
Opener tier
The model was 2 or more points off the opening line. It stays on the sheet while that gap is still on offer.
Wind tier
NFL unders in games forecast to be windy.
Model tier
The model disagrees with today's line. On the NFL board these are listed as Model leans and carry no stake.
Verdict
The board's read on timing for a row: bet now, or wait for a better number.
Line movement
Where each line opened and where it is now. Live means the edge is still there, Partly gone means some of it has been bet away, and Missed means the market has moved to the model.
Best bets
The short card of bets to place this week, also a PDF. Its results are kept as the Best Bets record.
Model leans
What the model thinks about a game where that opinion is not a bet. No stake.
Haircut
The share of the model's claimed edge that has shown up in graded bets. Stakes are sized from that share, not from the full claim.
Record-adjusted EV
A model EV counted only at the haircut's share. It is the figure shown first wherever an EV comes from the model.
Bucket stake
The stake for an opener or wind bet. It rests on that kind of bet's own record, not on the model being right about this game.
Live record
Every bet the Bet sheet flagged, at the price it was first flagged at, graded after the game. Also called the ledger.
Kept and withdrawn
A flagged bet still on the sheet at kickoff was kept. One the board took off before kickoff was withdrawn. The two are recorded separately.
Backtest
The model run over past seasons as if each week were live, to see how its bets would have done.
Tuning seasons and holdout
A change is fitted on the tuning seasons and then judged on the holdout, seasons it never saw.
Keep rule
A model change is kept only if the rule was written before the test and it is better on every measure, on the holdout by at least one standard error.
Provisional
Kept for now. It is checked again on the next full season it has not seen, and comes off if it fails.

Players and props

On the Players and Props sections, the matchup pages and the Prop Ladder.

Projection
The model's average expectation for a player's stat this week.
Typical
The model's own 50/50 number for a player this week: he is as likely to go over it as under it. It sits a little below the projection, because a few big games pull an average up.
Floor
The most yards he has reached in every game he played this season. If his lowest game is 41 receiving yards, his floor is 41.
Prop
A bet on one player's stat, such as over 62.5 receiving yards.
Main line
The standard line a book posts for a prop, priced near even money on both sides.
Alternate line
The same prop at a different number for a different price, such as 80+ receiving yards at +210.
Rung
One step on a ladder of alternate lines for the same stat: 40+, 50+, 60+ yards and so on. Each rung has its own price.
True probability
On the Prop Ladder: the chance of a rung, worked out from every book's price with the vig removed.
Fair odds
The price that matches the true probability exactly, with no vig.
Edge
On the Prop Ladder, true probability minus the chance the book's price implies. On the boards, the gap between the model and the line.
Injury tags
O out, D doubtful, Q questionable, P probable, IR injured reserve, PUP physically unable to perform, SUSP suspended.
WOPR
Weighted opportunity rating: a receiver's share of his team's targets and air yards, combined into one number.

Stats on the matchup pages

On CFB Matchups and NFL Matchups.

Expected points (EP)
Before each snap, the points an offense can expect from the drive, given the down, distance, field position and time left.
EPA
Expected points added: expected points after the play minus expected points before it. It measures how much one play helped or hurt.
EPA per play
A team's average EPA. For an offense higher is better; for a defense lower is better.
Success rate
The share of plays with positive EPA.
EPA per dropback
A quarterback's points added each time he drops back to pass, sacks and scrambles included.
CPOE
Completion percentage over expected: how often a quarterback completes passes compared with how hard those throws were.
Adjusted
On the EPA page: garbage time, kneels and spikes are left out, and every opponent's strength and home field are accounted for.
SRS
Simple Rating System: a team's strength in points per game against an average team, from points scored and allowed, adjusted for opponents. +7 is about a touchdown better than average.
OSRS and DSRS
The offense's and the defense's shares of SRS. Higher is better for both.
SoS
Strength of schedule: how strong a team's opponents have been, on the same points scale.
Implied total
The points each team is expected to score, worked out from the spread and the total. A 44.5 total with a 3-point favorite implies about 23.75 and 20.75.
Line move
How far the spread or total has moved since the opening line.
Defense vs position
How many yards and touchdowns a defense allows to running backs, wide receivers and tight ends, with its rank.

Records and how results are judged

On Recommended, the Weekly Review and the Model record.

Recommended list
A record of picks made under fixed rules, one rule per track, at $10 a pick.
Track
One rule on the Recommended list, such as NFL spreads at the opener. Every track starts On trial, and can be promoted only after 100 graded picks.
Weekly Review
A look back at every finished game: what the model got right, what it got wrong, and what came down to chance.
On the merits and by chance
In the Weekly Review, a result on the merits followed from how the game was played. One decided by chance turned on bounces such as turnovers returned for scores.
Met and Short
In the Weekly Review's player pages, whether a player reached his projection. A separate column counts how many reached Typical; about half should.
Standard error (±)
How much a result could wobble from luck alone. A return of +4% ± 4% is not yet distinguishable from zero.
Average miss
How far a projection was from the result, on average. Lower is better.
Brier score
A score for stated chances: the squared gap between each chance and what happened. Lower is better.
